# 🏞️ Riparian & Wetland Condition Tools

> [!abstract] In one line
> For the **water's edge and floodplain**, use purpose-built tools: **RARC** for rapid riparian condition, the **Index of Stream Condition** for reach-scale stream health, and the **MDB-specific** red gum/black box surveys + **Flow-MER** for environmental-watering outcomes.

Complements the upland-vegetation methods in [[Vegetation & Habitat Condition Monitoring]]; watering context in [[Regulation, Incentives & Markets]].

---

## Rapid Appraisal of Riparian Condition (RARC)
Scores riparian condition from **five sub-indices**:
1. **Habitat** — continuity/extent
2. **Cover** — vegetation cover & structural complexity
3. **Natives** — native vs exotic dominance
4. **Debris** — standing dead trees, hollows, fallen logs, leaf litter
5. **Features** — indicative features

Each is built from rapid field indicators — fast enough to apply over many reaches on a broad-scale property.
*Source: [RARC, Jansen et al. (Land & Water Australia)](https://riversofcarbon.org.au/wp-content/uploads/2019/07/Rapid-Appraisal-of-RIparian-Condition.pdf).*

> [!note] Current / updated versions
> - Original RARC (~2004) is dated, but regionally adapted versions exist (SA mid-north, Tasmanian midlands).
> - **RARCrec (2024)** updates the method for systems **undergoing recovery** — directly relevant to active restoration sites. *Source: [Duxbury et al. 2024, Ecological Management & Restoration](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/emr.12617).*
> - A current **Qld RARC v2** is maintained. *Source: [WetlandInfo RARC v2](https://wetlandinfo.des.qld.gov.au/wetlands/resources/tools/assessment-search-tool/rapid-appraisal-of-riparian-condition-rarc-v2/).*

## Index of Stream Condition (ISC) — Victoria
Scores the **"streamside zone"** sub-index from **9 riparian-vegetation indicators** — width, large trees, understorey lifeforms, recruitment, longitudinal continuity, tree canopy, litter, logs and weeds — benchmarked against the **EVC reference condition**.

Designed for **long-term assessment of rural stream reaches (10–30 km)** at **five-year intervals**; statewide benchmarks were completed in 1999, 2004 and 2010.
*Source: [An Index of Stream Condition: Reference Manual (Vic)](https://www.water.vic.gov.au/__data/assets/pdf_file/0027/673506/an-index-of-stream-condition-reference-manual.pdf); [ISC3 benchmark](https://www.water.vic.gov.au/our-programs/water-monitoring-and-reporting/third-index-of-stream-condition-report).*

> [!caution] Cadence may have lapsed
> The 5-year cycle is design intent; the most recent statewide benchmark confirmed is **2010** — re-benchmarking cadence may have lapsed or shifted to other programs.

## The Living Murray (TLM) — MDB tree condition 🌳
TLM assesses **river red gum** and **black box** condition via **ground survey of 30 trees per site**, scoring **crown extent** and **crown density**, plus trajectory indicators — new tip growth, epicormic growth, bark cracking, leaf die-off, mistletoe, reproductive activity, DBH and dominance class.

It also monitors woody-vegetation (river red gum, black box, coolabah) condition at **icon sites using RapidEye satellite imagery** (since ~2008), and a Basin-scale remote-sensing product tracks **tree water use** — pairing ground surveys with remote sensing.
*Sources: [Cunningham et al., TLM ground-based survey methods (MDBA)](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/292615732); [Red Gum Condition Index](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/abs/10.1080/03721426.2018.1557994); [MDBA Native vegetation](https://www.mdba.gov.au/managing-water/water-for-environment/native-vegetation).*

> [!tip] Borrow the 30-tree method
> Even outside formal TLM icon sites, the **30-trees-per-site crown extent + density** survey is a practical, repeatable way to track river red gum / black box health on your own floodplain.

## Flow-MER — environmental watering outcomes 💧
The Commonwealth **Flow-MER** program evaluates environmental-watering vegetation outcomes across **7 Selected Areas** using **field quadrat and transect surveys** (species-abundance data 2014–2023), with "Vegetation Diversity" and "Vegetation Condition" foci. Native species richness was **higher in environmentally-watered areas** than adjacent un-inundated areas.

Its non-woody framework groups species by **hydrological functional groups** (submerged, amphibious, damp-tolerant, etc.) tied to inundation regime, using **repeat annual/seasonal surveys aligned to watering events** (intervention monitoring).
*Sources: [Flow-MER Native Vegetation theme](https://www.flow-mer.org.au/basin-themes/vegetation); [vegetation species-abundance data](https://researchdata.edu.au/flow-mer-vegetation-species-abundance/2995492); [Flow-MER research (CSIRO)](https://research.csiro.au/flow-mer/research/).*

> [!caution] Verify schedule
> Functional-group framing and exact survey timing are partly from search extracts — confirm in the per-Selected-Area MER technical protocols.

## Putting it together for a riverine property

> [!tip] Suggested riparian monitoring stack
> 1. **RARC (or RARCrec)** across all reaches — rapid, repeatable, whole-of-property coverage.
> 2. **ISC streamside-zone** style assessment on major reaches every ~5 years for trend.
> 3. **30-tree red gum/black box** crown surveys on the floodplain.
> 4. **Event-based quadrats** (Flow-MER style) around any environmental watering, grouped by hydrological function.
> 5. **eDNA + waterbird/frog surveys** for the aquatic community → [[Fauna Monitoring Techniques]].
> 6. **Satellite NDVI / RapidEye-style** change detection over the floodplain → [[Remote Sensing & Data Standards]].
