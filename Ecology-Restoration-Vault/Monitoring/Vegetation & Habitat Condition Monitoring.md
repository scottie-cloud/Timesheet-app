# 🌿 Vegetation & Habitat Condition Monitoring

> [!abstract] In one line
> **Plot-based condition frameworks** (BAM, BioCondition, Habitat Hectares) score a site's native vegetation against a **reference benchmark** for its vegetation class. Add **fixed photo-points** and, on broad-scale land, **Landscape Function Analysis** and **standardised plots (TERN AusPlots)**.

The benchmark logic ties straight back to [[Restoration Planning & Standards (SERA)|the reference ecosystem]]; cadence is in [[Monitoring Schedules & Triggers]]. Riparian/wetland-specific tools are in [[Riparian & Wetland Condition Tools]].

---

## Why benchmarking
All the major frameworks below share a **reference-condition (benchmark) approach**: a site's current attributes are scored as a **proportion of an undisturbed reference state** for its vegetation class (PCT / Regional Ecosystem / EVC). That's how you set a baseline and measure recovery.

---

## Plot/transect condition frameworks

### NSW — Biodiversity Assessment Method (BAM) 2020
Measures **"vegetation integrity"** — condition scored across three categories of plot attributes (**composition, structure, function**), benchmarked against a reference ("best-on-offer") state for the **Plant Community Type (PCT)**. Composition and structure are captured as **richness and cover** of native species within growth-form groups (trees, shrubs, grasses, etc.).

- Plot-based floristic survey in a **20 m × 20 m plot**; **litter cover** averaged from five **1 m × 1 m** quadrats along a central **50 m** midline; **hollow-bearing trees** (hollow ≥5 cm opening, ≥1 m above ground) counted as a function attribute.
*Sources: [BAM 2020 Operational Manual (Stage 3)](https://www.environment.nsw.gov.au/sites/default/files/biodiversity-assessment-method-2020-operational-manual-stage-3-200584.pdf); [NSW BAM](https://www.environment.nsw.gov.au/topics/animals-and-plants/biodiversity-offsets-scheme/about/biodiversity-assessment-method).*

> [!note] Restoration-focused application
> The **NSW Biodiversity Conservation Trust** now runs an **Ecological Monitoring Module (EMM)** (operational manual **Sept 2024**) using BAM-derived vegetation-integrity plots to track condition on **private conservation/restoration land** — i.e. BAM plots applied directly to broad-scale property restoration.
> *Source: [BCT EMM Operational Manual (2024)](https://www.bct.nsw.gov.au/sites/default/files/2024-09/emm-operational-manual-sept-2024.pdf).*

> [!caution] Verify plot layout
> Exact composite plot layout (20×20 m floristic nested in a longer transect for structure/function) is partly from search snippets — confirm against the manual before quoting precisely.

### Queensland — BioCondition
A **site-based, quantitative, repeatable** framework producing a **numeric score** of a site's capacity to support biodiversity relative to the **Regional Ecosystem's reference ("best-on-offer") state**.

- **Nested plot 100 m × 50 m**: native **tree** richness across the full plot; **shrubs/grasses/forbs** in a 50 m × 10 m plot; **coarse woody debris (CWD)** in a 50 m × 20 m sub-plot (25–75 m along transect). CWD = total length of woody debris >10 cm diameter, >0.5 m long, >80% ground contact.
- Scores condition attributes against **Regional-Ecosystem benchmarks** — site attributes include large trees (default 30 cm DBH eucalypts / 20 cm non-eucalypts where no benchmark), canopy cover & height, recruitment, native richness by **four life-form groups**, CWD, litter and non-native cover.
*Sources: [BioCondition (Qld Govt)](https://www.qld.gov.au/environment/plants-animals/biodiversity/biocondition); [BioCondition Assessment Manual v2.2 (2015)](https://www.qld.gov.au/__data/assets/pdf_file/0029/68726/biocondition-assessment-manual.pdf).*

> [!caution] Version & scoring
> Manual v2.2 dates to **2015** (current standard but not "new"). Scoring totals are reported as **/50** (30 site + 20 landscape) in v2.2 snippets vs **/100** in other refs — verify the version's scoring table.

### Victoria — Vegetation Quality Assessment ("Habitat Hectares")
The standard Victorian approach: scores native vegetation **quality** by combining **site condition with patch context**, compared to an **Ecological Vegetation Class (EVC) benchmark**. Requires an **accredited assessor**.
*Source: [Vegetation Quality Assessment Manual v1.3 (DEECA)](https://www.environment.vic.gov.au/__data/assets/pdf_file/0016/91150/Vegetation-Quality-Assessment-Manual-Version-1.3.pdf). (v1.3 predates 2018 but remains the current Victorian standard.)*

### Landscape Function Analysis (LFA) — function on broad-scale land
Uses **11 rapidly field-assessed soil-surface indicators** (along transects with nested patch/inter-patch observations) to generate three indices — **stability, infiltration, nutrient cycling** — to track land function and recovery. Well suited to rangeland / grazing-land / rehabilitation monitoring.
*Source: [LFA Manual, Tongway & Hindley (CSIRO, 2004)](https://farmingtogether.com.au/wp-content/uploads/2023/11/Landscape_Function_Analysis_Procedures_for_Monitor.pdf); validated for grazing-land restoration in [Read et al. 2016](https://bsssjournals.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1111/ejss.12352). (2004 — dated but still widely used.)*

## Standardised plots — TERN AusPlots
**TERN AusPlots** establishes **~1 ha permanent plots** using **point-intercept sampling at 1,010 points** across ten 100 m transects in a grid, recording substrate, species, growth form and height — yielding standardised cover, floristic and structural data, applied at 700+ sites and revisited at least once a decade. A **directly transferable national standard** for broad-scale plot monitoring. → [[Remote Sensing & Data Standards]]
*Source: [TERN AusPlots / rangelands surveys](https://www.tern.org.au/news/ausplots-surveys-begin-in-the-rangelands/); [method paper](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC6128463/).*

## Fixed photo-points 📷
Cheap, powerful, and the easiest method to sustain on a working property.

**Standard practice:** photograph from precisely the same **marked location** (star post / permanent feature) and **bearing**, include a **distinctive orientation feature**, and **repeat at intervals matched to expected change** — typically **every 12 months to 3 years** for revegetation/grazing-exclusion responses.
*Sources: [NSW Guide to photo monitoring of ecological restoration projects (DPE, 2025 reprint)](https://www.environment.nsw.gov.au/sites/default/files/2025-04/restoration-rehabilitation-ecological-monitoring-guide-180068_0.pdf); [Connecting Country photo-point monitoring](https://connectingcountry.org.au/education-resources/monitor-change/photo-point-monitoring/).*

> [!tip] TERN panoramic photopoint standard
> TERN places **three photopoints in a 2.5 m equilateral triangle** around plot centre (star-dropper marked, GNSS/DGPS-located), each capturing a **360° panorama** of up to 40 overlapping (≥50% overlap) photos — a reusable, rigorous standard if you want repeatable panoramas.
> *Source: [TERN Photopoint Panoramic Images metadata](https://portal.tern.org.au/metadata/TERN/fe390ffb-8f30-44e2-872d-93fa96ec8f79).*

## What gets measured (the common attributes)
Across these frameworks, the recurring **floristic & structural attributes** are: native species richness (by growth form), projective/canopy cover, large/hollow-bearing trees, **recruitment**, coarse woody debris & fallen timber, litter, and **weed/non-native cover**. These map onto the SERA "structural diversity" and "species composition" attributes. → [[Restoration Planning & Standards (SERA)]]

## Choosing for your property

> [!tip] Quick guide
> - **In NSW** → BAM / BCT EMM plots (especially if engaging with offsets or [[Regulation, Incentives & Markets|biodiversity markets]]).
> - **In Qld** → BioCondition.
> - **In Vic** → Vegetation Quality Assessment (Habitat Hectares).
> - **Broad-scale / cross-jurisdiction / rangeland feel** → TERN AusPlots + LFA for function.
> - **Everywhere, always** → fixed photo-points as the cheap continuous backbone.
