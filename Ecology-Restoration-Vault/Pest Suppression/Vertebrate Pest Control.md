# 🦊 Vertebrate Pest Control

> [!abstract] In one line
> **Coordinated, neighbour-inclusive, landscape-scale, well-timed control beats isolated effort** for every species — and an **integrated toolkit** (bait + shoot + trap + habitat manipulation) is the consistent best-practice message. Work within each species' **Code of Practice / SOP** and confirm state **1080/PAPP licensing** and riparian setbacks first.

Effectiveness monitoring ties to [[Monitoring Schedules & Triggers]] and [[Fauna Monitoring Techniques|camera-trap occupancy]]; regulation in [[Regulation, Incentives & Markets]]. National hub: **[PestSmart (CISS)](https://pestsmart.org.au/)**.

> [!danger] Regulated & state-variable
> **1080/PAPP licensing differs by state** — NSW requires AQF3 Chemical Accreditation or Vertebrate Pesticide Induction Training (*Pesticide Control (1080 Bait Products) Order 2020*); Victoria requires an **ACUP** with 1080/PAPP endorsement or a Commercial Operator Licence. **Bait setbacks** (distance from water, dwellings, boundaries) are mandated and vary — **confirm current permit conditions before baiting near riparian zones.** *Sources: [NSW — Using 1080 baits](https://www.nsw.gov.au/regional-and-primary-industries/biosecurity/pest-animals/vertebrate-pests/using-1080-baits-nsw); [Vic ACUP](https://agriculture.vic.gov.au/farm-management/chemicals/licences-and-permits/agricultural-chemical-user-permit).*

## 🦊 Red fox

- **Coordinated, landscape-scale baiting outperforms isolated efforts.** Bait **twice yearly** — **late winter/spring** (breeding) and **autumn** (juvenile dispersal); outcomes improve when **neighbours also bait**. *Source: [PestSmart — Coordinated group fox programs](https://pestsmart.org.au/case_studies/coordinated-group-fox-programs/).*
- **Ground 1080 baiting** follows a national SOP; avoid urban/residential risk zones; time to lambing/dispersal. *Source: [NATSOP-FOX001](https://pestsmart.org.au/toolkit-resource/ground-baiting-of-foxes-with-sodium-fluoroacetate/).*
- **PAPP** is a registered alternative — **FOXECUTE** baits carry 400 mg PAPP in a 35 g meat bait; PAPP **has an antidote** (unlike 1080). *Source: [NATSOP-FOX007](https://pestsmart.org.au/toolkit-resource/baiting-of-foxes-with-para-aminopropiophenone-papp/).*
- **Den fumigation** with carbon monoxide is the only registered fumigant — humane if cubs >4 weeks, best when vixen+cubs confined to natal dens (≈Aug–Oct). *Source: [NATSOP-FOX004](https://pestsmart.org.au/toolkit-resource/fumigation-of-fox-dens-using-carbon-monoxide/).*
- **Integrate**: baiting + trapping + shooting + den fumigation/destruction + exclusion fencing. *Source: [Agriculture Victoria — Integrated fox control](https://agriculture.vic.gov.au/biosecurity/pest-animals/invasive-animal-management/integrated-fox-control).*
- **Map & coordinate** with **FoxScan** (FeralScan). *Source: [PestSmart — FoxScan](https://pestsmart.org.au/toolkit-resource/map-and-monitor-with-foxscan/).*

## 🐗 Feral pigs

- **National Feral Pig Action Plan** (2021, "PIGSTEP") — first national strategy; aims for sustained, coordinated, landscape-scale action. Up to ~24 million pigs occupy ~45% of the mainland, costing agriculture >$100M/yr. *Source: [feralpigs.com.au](https://feralpigs.com.au/); [plan PDF](https://pestsmart.org.au/wp-content/uploads/sites/3/2022/05/National-Feral-Pig-Action-Plan-A4.pdf).*
- **Toxic baits:** **PIGOUT/Econobait** (1080; 70 g bait, 24 mg 1080) and **HOGGONE** (sodium nitrite/meSN), which induces unconsciousness then death typically within 1–3 hrs. *Source: [NATSOP-PIG006 HOGGONE](https://pestsmart.org.au/toolkit-resource/baiting-hoggone-for-feral-pigs/).*
- **HogHopper** bait-delivery device has cut pig activity **70–90%** in field studies and excludes non-target species. *Source: [CISS — HOGGONE](https://invasives.com.au/our-solutions/tools-products/hoggone-feral-pig-baits/).*
- **Aerial (helicopter) shooting — increasingly thermal-assisted** — best in extensive/inaccessible terrain (swamps, marshes, broadacre crops). *Source: [NATSOP-PIG002](https://pestsmart.org.au/toolkit-resource/aerial-shooting-of-feral-pigs/).*
- **Trapping** requires **pre-feeding** (free-feed non-toxic bait several days) before setting; integrate with shooting + baiting. *(Remote-trigger corral traps with cellular cameras — e.g. "Jager" — are a current commercial refinement of this principle.)* *Source: [NATSOP-PIG001](https://pestsmart.org.au/toolkit-resource/trapping-of-feral-pigs/).*

## 🐰 European rabbit

- **Warren ripping/destruction is the most effective long-term control** (removes harbour) — make it the backbone, then follow with baiting/fumigation. *Source: [PestSmart — Rabbit toolkit](https://pestsmart.org.au/toolkits/rabbit/).*
- **RHDV1 K5** (released nationally from 2017) needs **>30× less virus** to infect than the older Czech strain and can overcome partial protection from benign RCV-A1 — **significant in cooler/temperate regions** where RCV-A1 is common. *Source: [NATSOP-RAB009](https://pestsmart.org.au/toolkit-resource/bait-delivery-of-rabbit-haemorrhagic-disease-virus-rhdv1-k5-strain/).*
- **Ground baiting:** **pindone** (anticoagulant; antidote = vitamin K1) suits lower-hazard areas near settlement; **1080** also registered. *Source: [NATSOP-RAB003 pindone](https://pestsmart.org.au/toolkit-resource/ground-baiting-of-rabbits-with-pindone/).*
- **Diffusion fumigation** of warrens — registered follow-up after baiting/biocontrol. *Source: [NATSOP-RAB004](https://pestsmart.org.au/toolkit-resource/diffusion-fumigation-of-rabbit-warrens/).*
- **Map & coordinate** with **RabbitScan**. *Source: [PestSmart — RabbitScan](https://pestsmart.org.au/toolkit-resource/rabbitscan/).*

## 🦌 Feral deer — emerging riparian threat

- **National Feral Deer Action Plan 2023–28** (Aug 2023) — 22 actions: halt spread of large populations, **eradicate small isolated populations before they establish**, protect priority sites. Population ~1–2 million and rising; ~$91M/yr. *Source: [feraldeerplan.org.au](https://feraldeerplan.org.au/the-plan/); [plan PDF](https://feraldeerplan.org.au/wp-content/uploads/2023/08/National-Feral-Deer-Action-Plan-2023-28.pdf).*
- **Thermal-assisted aerial shooting** is best practice in challenging terrain and **dense riparian vegetation** (maximises detection + welfare); now used in 5 states/territories. *Source: [NATSOP-DEER001](https://pestsmart.org.au/toolkit-resource/aerial-shooting-of-feral-deer/).*
- **Why it matters here:** deer **foul and erode streambanks** (wallowing, crossings), contaminate water (faeces, trampling), and make waterways shallow/silty — a direct threat to MDB floodplain/riparian assets. *Source: [Feral Deer Plan — The Challenge](https://feraldeerplan.org.au/the-challenge/).*

> [!warning] Act early on deer
> Deer are the **fastest-emerging riparian threat**. Eradicating small/isolated mobs **before they establish** is far cheaper and more effective than managing an entrenched population.

## 🐈 Feral cats — the hardest

- Broad-scale control is intrinsically difficult — **low density, large home ranges, wary, reluctant to take carrion baits** except when prey is scarce. **"No silver bullet"** — an integrated mix is required. *Source: [CSIRO Wildlife Research WR23107](https://www.publish.csiro.au/WR/WR23107).*
- **Felixer grooming traps** — automated, target-specific (rangefinder sensors distinguish cats/foxes from non-targets/humans), spray a measured toxic gel dose; solar-powered, 20 cartridges, auto-resetting; trialled across all states. *Source: [PestSmart — Felixer](https://pestsmart.org.au/toolkit-resource/felixer-feral-cat-grooming-traps-under-development/).*
- **Cat-specific baits:** **Eradicat** (4.5 mg 1080 sausage) and **Curiosity** (PAPP in an acid-soluble Hard Shell Delivery Vehicle) — mainly conservation/threatened-species programs; most effective broad-scale only when **alternative prey is minimal**. *Source: [NATSOP-CAT004](https://pestsmart.org.au/toolkit-resource/baiting-of-feral-cats-with-papp/).*

> [!note] Felixer status
> Described as "under development" on some PestSmart pages, but peer-reviewed **operational field trials** are published — treat as **operational-but-emerging**.

## 🧰 Cross-cutting best practice

- **Codes of Practice (COP) + Standard Operating Procedures (NATSOP/SOP)** exist per species and define the **humane, legal benchmark**; humaneness matrices rank methods. *Source: [PestSmart — feral pig COP](https://pestsmart.org.au/toolkit-resource/code-of-practice-feral-pigs/).*
- **Judas technique** — radio-collar a social animal, track it to the group; improves location/culling efficiency for **goats** (and adapted for **pigs** — ACT trials saw Judas sows contact 1–8 pigs within 1–7 days). Best for **low-density / eradication** phases. *Sources: [NATSOP-PIG004 Judas pigs](https://pestsmart.org.au/wp-content/uploads/sites/3/2024/05/NATSOP-PIG004.pdf); [NATSOP-GOA005 Judas goats](https://pestsmart.org.au/toolkit-resource/use-of-judas-goats/).*
- **Monitor effectiveness** — **spotlight counts, activity/abundance indices, camera-trap occupancy, FeralScan** records should **bracket control** (before & after) to measure knockdown and trigger follow-up. → [[Monitoring Schedules & Triggers]] *Source: [PestSmart — monitoring techniques](https://pestsmart.org.au/pest-animals/monitor-techniques/).*

## Synthesis for a riverine/floodplain property

> [!tip] The six takeaways
> 1. **Coordinate** — neighbour-inclusive, landscape-scale, twice-yearly timed control beats isolated effort, every species.
> 2. **Integrate** — bait + shoot + trap + habitat manipulation (warren ripping / den fumigation / exclusion).
> 3. **Thermal-assisted aerial shooting** is now standard for **pigs and deer** in dense riparian vegetation.
> 4. **Feral deer** = fastest-emerging riparian threat → act while populations are small.
> 5. **Feral cats** = hardest broad-scale → Felixers + Curiosity/Eradicat are leading emerging tools, no single method suffices.
> 6. **Stay legal** — work within species COP/SOPs; confirm state 1080/PAPP licensing and riparian setbacks before baiting near water.
