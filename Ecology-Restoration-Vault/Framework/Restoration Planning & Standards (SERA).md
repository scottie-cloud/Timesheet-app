# 🏗️ Restoration Planning & Standards (SERA)

> [!abstract] In one line
> The **SERA National Standards** are Australia's authoritative best-practice framework. They tell you to (1) define a **native reference ecosystem**, (2) match your **inputs to the level of degradation**, (3) set **clear measurable targets**, and (4) track recovery against **six ecosystem attributes** using the **five-star Recovery Wheel**.

Part of the [[Adaptive Management Framework]]; feeds [[Monitoring Schedules & Triggers]].

---

## The framework

Australia's national best-practice reference is the **National Standards for the Practice of Ecological Restoration in Australia**, published by the **Society for Ecological Restoration Australasia (SERA)** (a 2nd edition is current). Its international parallel is **Gann et al. (2019)**, *Restoration Ecology* 27(S1).
*Sources: [SERA Standards](https://www.seraustralasia.com/standards/standards.html); [Gann et al. 2019](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/rec.13035).*

The Standards define ecological restoration as *"the process of assisting the recovery of an ecosystem that has been degraded, damaged or destroyed,"* and require it to be based on an appropriate local **native (indigenous) reference ecosystem** representing the site's pre-degradation trajectory, **adjusted for environmental change including climate**.
*Source: [SERA Principle 1](https://www.seraustralasia.com/standards/principle1.html).*

### Guiding principles

The Standards are organised around a set of **key principles**, including:
1. Practice is based on an appropriate **native reference ecosystem**.
2. Restoration inputs are **dictated by the level of resilience and degradation**.
3. Recovery is facilitated by identifying **clear targets, goals and objectives**.
4. **Full recovery is the goal** wherever possible (even over long timeframes / high inputs).
5. **Stakeholder engagement** throughout.
6. Drawing on **best-available knowledge** (multiple lines of evidence, including Traditional Knowledge).

*Source: [SERA Standards](https://www.seraustralasia.com/standards/standards.html); [Principle 4](https://www.seraustralasia.com/standards/principle4.html).*

> [!caution] Verify the exact principle count/wording
> The SERA national edition and the SER **international** edition (which states "eight principles") differ. Confirm the precise wording/ordering of principles 5–6 against the SERA 2nd-edition PDF.

## The six ecosystem attributes

Recovery is described and tracked using **six key ecosystem attributes**, which together express ecosystem integrity:

1. **Absence of threats**
2. **Physical conditions**
3. **Species composition**
4. **Structural diversity**
5. **Ecosystem function**
6. **External exchanges** (landscape flows/connectivity)

*Source: [SERA Recovery Wheel](https://seraustralasia.com/wheel/); [SERA Principle 3](https://www.seraustralasia.com/standards/principle3.html).*

## The Recovery Wheel ⭐

The **Recovery Wheel** operationalises the six attributes via **18 sub-attributes** (three per attribute), each scored on a **five-star** scale. You shade segments to visualise the degree of recovery toward the reference ecosystem **over time** — a simple, powerful reporting graphic for a property.

> [!important] It is FIVE-star, not six-star
> The authoritative SERA system is a **five-star** rating across 18 sub-attributes. Treat any "six-star" reference (a common mix-up) as **incorrect**.

*Source: [SERA Recovery Wheel](https://seraustralasia.com/wheel/) (web/Excel/app versions available).*

## The restorative continuum

Not every hectare can be returned to a pristine native state. The **restorative continuum** orders activities by the degree of recovery achieved — useful for a working property with a mix of land conditions:

| Activity | What it means | Typical setting |
|---|---|---|
| Reduce impacts | Cut ongoing societal/ecological pressure | Whole-property |
| **Remediation** | Remove contaminants / threats | Degraded patches |
| **Rehabilitation** | Reinstate **function & services** (not full native recovery) | Ex-cropping, heavily modified production land |
| **Ecological restoration** | Assist recovery toward the **native reference** trajectory | Remnants, riparian corridors, priority assets |

Rehabilitation supports native biodiversity but does **not** achieve substantive recovery of a natural ecosystem.
*Sources: [SERA Appendix](https://www.seraustralasia.com/standards/appendix1.html) (drawing on Gann et al. 2019); [FAO restoration summary](https://openknowledge.fao.org/server/api/core/bitstreams/8e99e8b3-6cdd-4842-872a-2514ef17b709/content/delivering-restoration-outcomes-for-biodiversity-2024/what-is-restoration.html).*

## How this anchors your monitoring

All the major Australian plot-condition frameworks — **BAM, BioCondition, Habitat Hectares, the ISC streamside zone** — share this **reference-condition (benchmark) approach**: a site's current attributes are scored as a **proportion of an undisturbed reference state** for its vegetation class. So the SERA reference ecosystem and your field benchmarks are the same idea, operationalised. → [[Vegetation & Habitat Condition Monitoring]]
