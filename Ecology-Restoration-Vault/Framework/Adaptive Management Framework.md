# 🔄 Adaptive Management Framework

> [!abstract] In one line
> Run restoration as a **loop**: plan → act → monitor → evaluate → review/adjust. Set a **baseline first**, write **SMART goals** anchored to a [[Restoration Planning & Standards (SERA)|reference ecosystem]], and design monitoring so its data actually **changes** management decisions.

```
   ┌─────────► 1. PLAN ─────────► 2. ACT ─────────► 3. MONITOR ─┐
   │      (goals, baseline,     (on-ground         (fixed-cadence   │
   │       reference state)      works)             measurement)    │
   │                                                                ▼
   └──────── 5. REVIEW / ADJUST ◄──────────────────── 4. EVALUATE ◄─┘
              (change the plan)                       (vs targets & triggers)
```

---

## The cycle

Restoration should run a formal **adaptive management cycle — plan → implement/act → monitor → evaluate → review/adjust**. SERA states adaptive management *"can and should be a standard approach for any ecological restoration project irrespective of how well-funded."* Well-funded projects are expected to do **formal monitoring with a documented monitoring plan** (design, timeframes, responsibility, analysis, and a response framework).
*Source: [SERA Standards](https://www.seraustralasia.com/standards/standards.html).*

### The Open Standards (a practical scaffold)

The **Open Standards for the Practice of Conservation** (Conservation Measures Partnership) define a five-step adaptive cycle:
1. **Conceptualise** — vision & context
2. **Plan** — actions & monitoring
3. **Implement**
4. **Analyse, use, adapt**
5. **Capture & share learning**

In Australia these run as **Conservation Action Planning / Healthy Country Planning**, used by 20+ organisations across ~140 projects covering ~160 million ha — a proven, broad-scale-friendly planning method.
*Sources: [Open Standards (CMP)](https://www.cbd.int/doc/pa/tools/Open%20standards%20for%20the%20practice%20of%20conservation.pdf); [Bush Heritage — measuring impact](https://www.bushheritage.org.au/what-we-do/our-impact/measuring).*

> [!example] Full-cycle in practice
> **Boolcoomatta Reserve** (SA, Bush Heritage) cycled its management plan through **three major adaptations over nine years** of monitoring data — a worked example of full-cycle adaptive management in arid/semi-arid rangelands.
> *Source: [Conservation Standards library](https://www.conservationstandards.org/library-item/full-cycle-adaptive-management-in-australias-arid-rangelands/).*

## 1. PLAN

### Set the baseline first
**Baseline assessment before intervention** provides the benchmark against which all progress and impact are measured. You cannot demonstrate recovery without a measured starting point. Use the relevant plot/condition method for the asset:
- Native vegetation → [[Vegetation & Habitat Condition Monitoring]]
- Riparian/wetland → [[Riparian & Wetland Condition Tools]]
- Fauna → [[Fauna Monitoring Techniques]]
- Whole-of-property change → [[Remote Sensing & Data Standards]]

### Write SMART goals
Goals/objectives should be **SMART** — **S**pecific, **M**easurable, **A**chievable, **R**elevant, **T**ime-bound — anchored to the reference ecosystem, with **indicators/metrics identified at the outset**. *"Evaluation can only be as rigorous as the monitoring that informs it."*
*Source: [SERA Principle 4](https://www.seraustralasia.com/standards/principle4.html); [Principle 3](https://www.seraustralasia.com/standards/principle3.html).*

> [!tip] Worked example of a SMART restoration objective
> *"Increase native understorey species richness in the river red gum riparian zone (Reach 3) from a baseline of 8 to ≥18 species per [[Vegetation & Habitat Condition Monitoring|BioCondition]] plot within 5 years, while reducing exotic perennial-grass cover from 60% to <20%."*

### Whole-of-property / landscape planning
- Create a **whole-farm plan first**, then prioritise revegetation/restoration sites.
- Connect remnant patches via **corridors and stepping-stone pockets** for fauna movement and connectivity.
- Protect and re-establish **scattered paddock trees** by fencing and assisting natural regeneration — restores habitat without removing large areas from production. (NSW guidance suggests fencing individual paddock trees at least ~20 m × 20 m — *verify current figure for your jurisdiction*.)
*Sources: [NSW Govt — Planning a revegetation project](https://www.nsw.gov.au/regional-and-primary-industries/natural-resource-management/revegetating-your-property/planning-a-revegetation-project); [Wildlife corridors](https://www.nsw.gov.au/regional-and-primary-industries/natural-resource-management/revegetating-your-property/planting-for-wildlife-habitat-corridors); [ANU Sustainable Farms — Paddock trees](https://www.sustainablefarms.org.au/on-the-farm/paddock-trees/).*

## 2. ACT
On-ground works — staged by priority and asset value. For broad-scale properties the consistent best-practice message is **coordinate at landscape scale** and **integrate methods**:
- Pests → [[Vertebrate Pest Control]]
- Weeds → [[Invasive Weed Management]]
- Revegetation / natural regeneration → see whole-farm planning above
- Environmental watering integration (MDB) → [[Regulation, Incentives & Markets]]

---

## 3. MONITOR & 4. EVALUATE
Measure against your baseline and targets on a **fixed cadence**, then evaluate against **pre-agreed decision triggers**. Detailed cadences and trigger logic are in **[[Monitoring Schedules & Triggers]]**.

---

## 5. REVIEW / ADJUST — close the loop
Monitoring must be designed to **feed back into management**: the monitoring plan should specify design, timeframes, responsibility, planned analysis, and a documented **framework for response and communication**, so data **triggers management change** rather than sitting unused. Project documentation (objectives, reference model, baseline, indicators, results) is itself best practice.
*Sources: [SERA Standards](https://www.seraustralasia.com/standards/standards.html); [Open Standards (CMP)](https://www.cbd.int/doc/pa/tools/Open%20standards%20for%20the%20practice%20of%20conservation.pdf).*
