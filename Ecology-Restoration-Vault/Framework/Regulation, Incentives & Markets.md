# ⚖️ Regulation, Incentives & Markets

> [!abstract] In one line
> The policy scaffolding around broad-scale restoration: **EPBC** (what you must refer), the **Murray–Darling Basin Plan** environmental watering you can integrate with, the **Nature Repair Act** biodiversity market, and **Landcare/NRM** funding — plus the regulated pesticide rules that govern on-ground works.

Pairs with [[Adaptive Management Framework]]; pesticide-specific rules live in [[Vertebrate Pest Control]] and [[Invasive Weed Management]].

---

## Murray–Darling Basin environmental watering 💧

Under the **Basin Plan**, the MDBA's **Basin-wide Environmental Watering Strategy** (1st ed. 2014; **2nd ed. 22 Nov 2019**; updated 2024/25) sets long-term, quantified **"expected environmental outcomes"** across four components:
1. **River flows & connectivity**
2. **Native vegetation** — river red gum, black box, coolibah forests/woodlands, wetland vegetation
3. **Waterbirds**
4. **Native fish**

Outcomes are framed to be as **SMART as practicable**.
*Source: [MDBA Basin-wide Environmental Watering Strategy (2019 PDF)](https://www.mdba.gov.au/sites/default/files/publications/basin-wide-environmental-watering-strategy-november-2019_3.pdf); [strategy page](https://www.mdba.gov.au/publications-and-data/publications/basin-wide-environmental-watering-strategy).*

Environmental watering is delivered with **real-time adaptive management**: the Commonwealth Environmental Water Office's **Flow-MER** program (which replaced LTIM/EWKR) provides evidence on vegetation, fish, waterbirds and flow outcomes that feeds in-season delivery decisions, forward planning, and statutory reporting. → [[Riparian & Wetland Condition Tools]]
*Source: [DCCEEW/CEWO Flow-MER](https://www.dcceew.gov.au/water/cewo/monitoring/mer-program); [Flow-MER](https://www.flow-mer.org.au/about).*

> [!caution] Implementation reality check
> Independent analysis ("A trickle, not a flood," *Marine & Freshwater Research*) found only **~12%** of the river red gum area targeted by expected outcomes was actually flooded, with about half of those events likely sub-optimal — several Basin Plan vegetation/wetland outcomes were unlikely to be met by the 2024 milestone. Plan your own watering-dependent restoration goals with this delivery risk in mind.
> *Source: [Colloff et al., MF20172](https://www.publish.csiro.au/mf/mf20172).*

## EPBC Act (national environmental law) 🛡️

Under the **EPBC Act**, actions likely to have a **"significant impact" on a Matter of National Environmental Significance** require Commonwealth referral/approval. Routine agricultural activities are generally **exempt** *unless* they expand/intensify land use; clearing regrowth vegetation older than ~15 years may trigger self-assessment or referral — **relevant when restoration involves clearing, or affects listed species/communities**.
*Source: [DCCEEW Significant Impact Guidelines 1.1](https://www.dcceew.gov.au/environment/epbc/publications/significant-impact-guidelines-11-matters-national-environmental-significance); [What's protected](https://www.dcceew.gov.au/environment/epbc/our-role/what-is-protected).*

> [!caution] EPBC reform in flux
> Reforms (an "unacceptable impacts" test and restoration-contribution mechanisms) were still in legislative passage as of late 2025 / early 2026 — **re-check current status** before relying on them.

## Nature Repair Act 2023 — biodiversity market 🌱

The **Nature Repair Act 2023** (in force **15 Dec 2023**) established a national, legislated, **voluntary biodiversity market**: landholders running approved projects can earn **tradeable biodiversity certificates**. It is administered by the **Clean Energy Regulator** — which also runs the **ACCU/carbon** scheme, enabling **carbon + biodiversity "stacking."** Methods and a Biodiversity Assessment Instrument are being developed by DCCEEW.
*Source: [DCCEEW Nature Repair Market](https://www.dcceew.gov.au/environment/environmental-markets/nature-repair-market); [CER](https://cer.gov.au/news-and-media/news/2024/february/cer-commences-work-nature-repair-market).*

## Funding & incentives 💵

- **National Landcare Program** — on-ground restoration funding (Phase 2 invested >$1 billion to mid-2023; **Environment Small Grants $5,000–$50,000** at up to 100% of project cost).
- **State programs** — e.g. NSW Landcare Enabling Program 2023–2027; Victorian Landcare Grants ($3.98M across 245 grants in 2023) fund revegetation, fencing and monitoring.
- **Local Land Services (NSW) / Greening Australia** run landscape-scale connectivity programs (e.g. "Adaptive Farms for Sustainable Landscapes"; "Protecting and Connecting" woodlands).
*Sources: [National Landcare Program](https://www.nrm.gov.au/national-landcare-program); [Environment Small Grants](https://business.gov.au/grants-and-programs/national-landcare-program-environment-small-grants); [LLS Riverina](https://www.lls.nsw.gov.au/regions/riverina/projects-and-programs/adaptive-farms-for-sustainable-landscapes); [Greening Australia](https://www.greeningaustralia.org.au/projects/protecting-and-connecting-act-woodlands/).*

## Pesticide regulation (the part that bites on-ground) ⚠️

Two regulated areas govern most pest/weed works on a riverine property — covered in detail in the method notes:

- **1080 / PAPP vertebrate baiting** — licensing is **state-variable** (NSW accreditation/induction under the *Pesticide Control (1080 Bait Products) Order 2020*; Victoria's ACUP with 1080/PAPP endorsement), with mandated setbacks from water/dwellings/boundaries. → [[Vertebrate Pest Control]]
- **Herbicide use near water** — only **aquatic-registered** formulations/surfactants may be used over/beside water, often under **time-limited APVMA minor-use permits**; plus state pesticide-use law (e.g. NSW *Pesticides Act*, notification). → [[Invasive Weed Management]]

> [!danger] Always verify before acting
> Permits expire and labels change. Confirm **current** APVMA permits, product labels, and state codes of practice **before** any baiting or near-water herbicide application.
